# Organizational Overlay

Use this reference when the incident is not only a reporting problem but also a leadership, ownership, or operating-rhythm problem.

## Leadership Objective

Treat the incident process as a company capability with five leadership demands:

- **visionary leadership**: define the future-state standard for how the vendor handles live customer incidents
- **strategic leadership**: choose the changes that most improve trust, speed, and clarity
- **operational leadership**: establish clear roles, standards, and rhythms
- **people leadership**: reduce blame, increase ownership, and coach better behavior in the room
- **external leadership**: protect customer confidence and executive trust through calm, credible communication

## Operating Principles

Use these principles in incident reviews and follow-up:

- debate in private, unite in public
- challenge ideas, support people
- company first, function second
- transparency with trust
- accountability without blame

## Default Stakeholder Map

Map the incident across these groups:

- customer security team
- customer executive sponsor or final approver
- vendor security lead
- vendor technical or product lead
- vendor commercial lead
- vendor executive sponsor

Before drafting anything, decide:

- who needs detailed facts
- who needs reassurance
- who needs a decision
- who owns the next move

## Default Decision Rights

Unless the situation clearly requires otherwise:

- **security lead** owns incident facts, customer incident call structure, and final technical position
- **technical lead** owns platform truth, product gaps, and feasibility of changes
- **commercial lead** owns relationship tone and separate commercial follow-up, but does not run the incident narrative
- **executive sponsor** steps in for trust repair, senior stakeholder alignment, or material account risk

## Default Meeting Cadence

For meaningful customer incidents, use this rhythm:

- `T+0 to 2h`: incident lead named, stakeholder map set, first fact pattern assembled
- same day: first customer call and flash report or draft note
- `T+24h`: reconciled timeline and owner-based action tracker
- `T+48h`: internal review of misses, routing gaps, and process failures
- `T+7d`: leadership review of durable system changes, training, and follow-through

## Organizational Review Loop

After the immediate report is out, run a lightweight four-part review:

### 1. Assessment

- what behaviors helped
- what behaviors damaged trust
- where responsibilities blurred

### 2. Gap Analysis

- current versus desired operating model
- role gaps
- communication gaps
- system or workflow gaps

### 3. Design

- update runbooks
- update templates
- update role ownership
- define quick wins

### 4. Embedding

- train people on the new pattern
- reinforce it in future incidents
- check whether the new behavior actually happened

## What Good Looks Like

The process is improving when:

- the customer gets a calm same-day note with no visible internal confusion
- the team speaks with one voice externally
- actions have owners and dates within hours, not days
- commercial discussions are cleanly separated from live incident handling
- repeated failure modes turn into system changes, not one-off apologies
