# Incident Report Review Checklist

Use this checklist before sending a customer-facing incident report.

## Evidence discipline

- [ ] Confirmed facts are clearly separated from customer-reported facts.
- [ ] Open questions are visible.
- [ ] The timeline is internally consistent.
- [ ] Dates and times are exact where known.

## Tone

- [ ] The report does not read defensively.
- [ ] Vendor-owned gaps are stated plainly.
- [ ] The report is written for senior readers, not only analysts.
- [ ] Commercial points are not mixed into the incident narrative.

## Action quality

- [ ] Every recommendation has an owner.
- [ ] Every important action has a target timing.
- [ ] Immediate actions are separated from longer-term improvements.
- [ ] Cross-functional issues have named leadership ownership, not just task ownership.

## Organizational quality

- [ ] The report reflects one external company voice.
- [ ] Customer-facing incident handling is clearly separated from commercial follow-up.
- [ ] The process makes decision rights clear: security, technical, commercial, executive.
- [ ] Repeated failure modes are being turned into training, template, or runbook changes.

## Delivery quality

- [ ] The document status is right: flash, draft, or final.
- [ ] The executive summary can be read in under two minutes.
- [ ] The reader can tell what happened, what changed, and what happens next.
- [ ] The document looks like a current executive incident memo, not a generic cyber deck.
- [ ] The cover page is strong, but body pages stay light and easy to read unless there is a clear reason not to.
- [ ] No page is overcrowded or dependent on very small text.
- [ ] Dense text is not forced into narrow 3-column layouts.
- [ ] The action plan keeps a visible owner column.
- [ ] Technical labels are translated into plain English on first reference.
